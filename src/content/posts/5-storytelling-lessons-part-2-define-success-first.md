Every writing guru promises you a bestseller. McSill opens lesson 2 by saying the quiet part out loud: you can't engineer one.

I like him a little more for it.

Here's what the chapter says, minus the detours.

## The Book's Big Promise

Know what you want before you write. McSill says a bestseller happens; it can't be manufactured.

He also argues that measuring success against other people's sales is a trap. Even the official sales trackers are imprecise, and fair comparisons need similar books, publishers and distribution.

## The Filler You Can Skip

- **A dictionary definition of "success."** Yes, really.
- **The Paris-with-your-spouse-and-mother-in-law gag.** It's a joke about book money. Moving on.
- **A French pop song** the author uses as a life philosophy.
- **A checklist of 14 personal qualities** borrowed from Napoleon Hill, rated 0 to 10. Skim it. Don't spend your afternoon scoring yourself.

## The Golden Takeaway

### Redefine success

McSill defines success as changing at least one world, yours or someone else's, with the book you write.

That's his answer to the "what is a bestseller?" problem. Pick a definition you control, then aim at it.

### Run the vanity check

Are you writing because you have something to say, or because you'd love a launch party? His warning: some authors have little to say but adore the signing night.

If your motive is vanity, it's cheaper to just throw the party.

### Pick your message

He offers a short list of what a story-driven book can do for the reader:

- Point to a path to follow.
- Help them decide who they are.
- Shape their values and behaviors.
- Help them handle everyday experiences.
- Let them live someone else's experience and see the world differently.

Pick one as your main job.

### Write the pitch and the bookends

- Write a central idea in about 45 words, plus a working title.
- Write the opening and ending you _imagine_. McSill says this speeds up the whole book.

### Stop talking, start writing

He insists on a "leap of faith": writing beats announcing. Better to find out you can't do it than to wonder forever.

## Skip the Book, Do This

Set a timer for five minutes. Write your book or project idea in 45 words or fewer, then add one sentence: _"My reader will change by..."_

If the sentence won't come, you've found your homework.
