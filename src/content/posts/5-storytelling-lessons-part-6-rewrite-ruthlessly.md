## The Book's Big Promise

Writing is rewriting. Treat your first pass as "version zero" and expect to edit it hard.

The lesson ends with templates and a bonus section of real manuscript critiques, so you can see edits in action.

## The Filler You Can Skip

- **Voice quizzes** that ask you to match dialect to character. Fun, but optional.
- **Excerpts from published authors** you're asked to analyze. Do one, not all.
- **Bonus critiques** are long. Read one or two.
- **The author's farewell interview** at the end, unless you're a fan.

## The Golden Takeaway

### Write like a film director

You can only film what's visible. So write actions and consequences, not interior monologue. If it can't be shot, show it through behavior.

### Five rewrite criteria

1. **Don't be too clever.** Use simple language and a simple structure. Write to deliver a message, not to show off.
2. **Don't copy reality as it is.** Skip sunrise descriptions, passive characters, and slow warm-ups. Everything needs a cause.
3. **Hold your point of view.** Know whose scene it is. Don't lecture readers or insert yourself.
4. **Don't be embarrassed** to be obvious, use "said," plan your scenes, show first drafts, or be edited heavily.
5. **Don't over-criticize yourself.** Show work to non-experts, and drop ideas that complicate the story.

### Cut, then cut again

McSill says most plots can lose 20 to 40% of their content. Keep every version so you can compare. And make sure each emotional beat has a transition leading into it.

### Find the voice

His exercise: describe your evening in 40 to 55 words without _to be_ verbs. That's your voice. Now rewrite the same events in a character's voice.

Prefer concrete images over flat statements. "Maria cried" tells; a specific image shows.

### Templates, not straitjackets

- **How-to book:** roughly 10% opening, 75% body (at least one short story per chapter), 10% conclusion, 5% extras.
- **Novel:** plan before you write. Skip planning and you'll rewrite three or four times.

## Skip the Book, Do This

Take one paragraph from your draft and cut it by a third. Check it against criterion 2: does every sentence have a reason to exist?

Keep the short version.

## Series Wrap-Up

Here's the whole book in six lines:

1. You're born a storyteller. Open a gap and keep it open.
2. Define your own success before you write.
3. Story = want + obstacle + change.
4. Plan from the ending; keep one point of view.
5. Pain, then relief, drives readers. Ask "how so?" of hype.
6. Cut hard.

That's 398 pages in 60 seconds. Go write something.
